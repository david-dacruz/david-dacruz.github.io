Field note 03 / Search systems

Technical SEO needs evidence, not more dashboards

A traffic chart can show that something changed. It cannot tell you whether the cause is crawling, rendering, templates, intent, competition, or links.

David Dacruz9 August 20266 minute read

On this page

Turn the symptom into a testable question

“Traffic is down” is a symptom. A useful diagnostic question names a page set, a date range, a search surface, and a suspected mechanism.

For example: did product pages lose indexed URLs after a template release, or did impressions remain stable while click-through rate fell? Those questions require different evidence and different owners.

Check the system in layers

  1. Discovery: can crawlers find the intended URLs?
  2. Access: are status codes, robots rules, and canonicals coherent?
  3. Rendering: does the meaningful content exist in the delivered page?
  4. Indexing: which representative URLs are indexed, excluded, or canonicalised elsewhere?
  5. Intent: does the page solve the query better than the current result set?
  6. Authority: is there internal and external evidence that the page deserves attention?

Use representative URLs before exporting everything

Choose a small sample across page types and states: successful, declining, new, excluded, duplicated, and strategically important. Inspect them directly before building another aggregate report.

Keep the evidence chain: URL, observed state, source, date, expected state, owner, proposed change, and verification result.

Change one system and verify the result

Template-wide fixes should be separated from page-specific editorial work. Link acquisition should not be used to hide an indexing fault, and another content rewrite will not repair contradictory canonicals.

After release, verify the affected URLs and the mechanism you intended to change. The reporting view comes after the technical check, not instead of it.

Technical SEO FAQ

Questions about evidence-led SEO diagnosis

What is the first step in a technical SEO diagnosis?

Turn the symptom into a testable question that names the affected page set, date range, search surface, and suspected mechanism.

Which layers should a technical SEO audit check?

Check discovery, access, rendering, indexing, intent, and authority in sequence so each observation can be connected to the correct system and owner.

Why inspect representative URLs before exporting every report?

A representative sample exposes concrete crawl, render, canonical, index, content, and authority states before aggregate reporting hides the mechanism.

How should a technical SEO fix be verified?

Inspect the affected URLs after release and verify the exact mechanism changed as intended before relying on aggregate reporting.
